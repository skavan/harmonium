# Device takeover — the cast hands you the device's own remote

Status: **BUILT (2026-09-04, same day).** The smallest honest slice shipped: a pre-wired device's generated page now speaks the device — context, dialect, keys, chrome. Fenced by `probe-takeover-chrome.mjs`. Prompted by a forum user's comment during 0.87 packaging: the device pages reached from an activity's cast offer only a few generic functions, so adjusting anything real on a cast member still means fishing out its old remote. Suresh's framing question: "What is he asking for that we already don't do?" — answered in the next section: most of the pieces existed; the gap was that nothing connected them automatically, and closing it took two small engine changes (§Sequencing).

## The ask (a forum user, verbatim)

> Given you are in an active activity, and you select one of the devices in the cast from the activity, then the functions of the remote change to the roles of the pre-wired device (not the activity) until returning to the activity page.
>
> Example: My Watch TV activity gives the D-Pad role to a Fire TV and the volume role to an AVR and no roles to the Sony TV, but if I click on the Sony TV from the cast, all roles switch to the Sony TV and I can use the button map (physical remote or phone) for the D-pad and the volume on the Sony TV.

## What already exists (the gap analysis, measured against src as of 0.87)

- **"Define what we could use in those device pages" — exists.** Every domain's device page is an editable controller: `config.controllers.<domain>` with tiles bound to `$device` is the editable form of the built-in DETAIL_TILES composition, and a per-device custom copy (`variant_of` + `entity`) wins for its one device (details.js `detailDef`/`bindDeviceTiles`; surfaced in the Studio's library). A media player's page already carries power, transport, a volume stepper, input chips, and sound-mode chips.
- **Volume following the viewed device — exists, on the detail page.** The VOL keys already retarget to the device's primary range on its detail screen only (`DETAIL_VOL_KIND`: a media player's volume, a light's brightness, a cover's position); everywhere else VOL stays room/activity audio, by design.
- **A page taking over the physical keys — the mechanism exists, for authored pages.** A screen can declare `control_target` with `pass_through` (v2 input policy; v1 `dpad_passthrough`), and the physical d-pad, back/home/menu, short-press power, and the tuner's channel keys all drive that entity from that page — input.js calls this the Harmony rule: "physical D-pad drives the device when the view says so." So a hand-built "Sony TV" page can already do most of the ask.
- **The actual gap — none of it is automatic from the cast.** Tapping a cast member opens the generated `detail:<entity>` page, which (a) never declares a control target, so the physical keys stay with the activity's roles, and (b) is a page about ONE entity, not the pre-wired device — the device's `roles` map, its `dpad_commands`, and its dialect capabilities are never bundled in. The takeover today requires authoring a page by hand and naming entities yourself; the pre-wired device already knows everything that page would say.

So the build is one connection, not a feature: cast tap → the DEVICE's page (not a bare entity's), with the control target derived from `CONFIG.devices[id].roles` and the device's dialect — and the handoff made visible.

## Why this shape is right

- **It is Harmony's "device mode" without the mode.** Harmony solved the same itch with a Devices button and a modal state users stranded themselves in. Here the scope is the page: you enter by navigating to the device, you leave by navigating back, and there is nothing to remember and nothing to un-stick. The law in one sentence: **the page you are standing on owns the keys.**
- **No new authoring.** A pre-wired device already IS an assembly of roles plus a dialect (design-device-capabilities: "the 'device' you point the remote at is an assembly of roles, never a single entity"). The Sony carries its own dpad/volume/source wiring whether or not any activity grants it a role — that wiring is exactly what the takeover activates. Nothing per-activity to configure, no second keymap to maintain.
- **Activity state is untouched.** Routing, ownership, the room select — none of it moves. This is purely input routing, which is why it is cheap and why it can never break "ending Watch TV never kills the Music."

## Mechanics — why the plumbing mostly exists

- context.js already composes `$context` as the screen's context overlaid by the active activity's. The takeover is one more rung: while standing on a cast device's page, `$context` answers from `CONFIG.devices[id].roles` and the active dialect is the device's own. Everything downstream then follows for free: `boundButtons()`'s dialect rung serves the DEVICE's capabilities (a tizen Settings, not firetv's), `cmdFor` speaks the device's own `dpad_commands`, the volume keys ride the device's volume role, the source chooser its `source_select`.
- The on-screen button map follows the same switch — his "physical remote or phone" phrasing is one mechanism, not two: the tiles the page draws read the same `$context`, so the detail page can grow from today's generic primitives toward the device's full role set drawn in the existing control language. No new shapes.
- A role the device does not wire keeps the deliberate-no-op law (the presence gate in design-device-capabilities §6). A cast member that is a bare entity rather than a pre-wired device has nothing to take over — its page stays exactly today's detail page.

## The laws it must keep (Suresh's banner ruling, 2026-09-04)

1. **The handoff is visible — via the existing passthrough chrome.** The engine already answers this: `passthroughActive()` lights the bar's passthrough cue (accent rule + gamepad glyph) and pins the BACK / HOME strip (`tvstrip`). Suresh: "on *any* device that takes over the dpad etc we insert the BACK HOME banner" — which is exactly the strip's existing gate, so every takeover page gets it for free. Two refinements to make the nesting legible (his "How does the user know they are in a nested device?"): the strip's center wordmark names who holds the keys — the device's name instead of HARMONIUM — and the title bar wears a treatment while takeover is live, leaning the device's accent wash (the identity palette's "color is identity" law, no new vocabulary). Remotes without the strip keep the bar cue.
2. **You can always get home.** The takeover ends by leaving the page, so the exit must never be capturable by the device — the strip and the hold gesture guarantee it (see the back doctrine below).
3. **Battery and the floor.** No new radio, no new server surface, one context rung in the engine; Chromium-61 safe.

## Ruled and built the same day: dialect is a DEVICE property (2026-09-04)

Suresh, closing the round-3 loop: "dialect is an activity property AND in device mode a device property. You could argue its a device property everywhere and then in roles, you pick the device from the cast (default is primary). That's probably the way to go." Built as ruled: the dialect NAME lives on the device (the Devices editor's knob, now also a Dialect picker in the cast member's ⚙ — offered only to devices with a role a dialect speaks through); the cast row wears the device's dialect as a quiet chip; and the activity's Roles "Dialect" row is now a WHOSE-VOICE pick — a cast device, default the primary — stored as `wiring.dialect` (a device id, never a dialect name) and resolved by `compileContext`. A voiceless pick falls back to the primary, never guesses. The legacy pinned name (`overrides.dialect`) still reads, labeled as legacy, and is released by choosing a device. The takeover page and the activity now draw their voice from the same field, which structurally prevents the round-3 leak from ever mattering again. Alongside it, the stock **tizen** dialect gained the Samsung `KEY_*` `dpad_commands` (starter + stocklib twins, stock-history regenerated), so "this device speaks Samsung Tizen" is a complete sentence — pick the dialect and the keys come with it. Fenced by `probe-voice-device.mjs`.

Migration is FORCED, through the house heal (Suresh: "should we … have users never migrating — or force the upgrade?" → force): `healPinnedDialects` runs on Studio load and moves each legacy pinned name in three rungs — a cast device already speaking it becomes the voice pick; a voiceless primary (else dpad) device adopts it as its own dialect; only a genuinely ambiguous pin stays, honored, labeled legacy, and counted in the status line so it is a loose end, never a resting place. Compiled behavior is byte-identical for the healed rungs.

Round 4 refinements (2026-09-04, field): the page title and the strip both wear the **display-name chain** — the running activity's ⚙ Display name → the device's library name → the entity's friendly name — one chain, so they can never disagree ("we show the unfriendly entity name … show whatever ends up as the Display Name"). And a takeover page **inherits its opener's bindings**: an app-level binding like the TV controller's menu → apps rides onto the device page (his Samsung's menu key was opening the TV's own settings), while `$context`-relative bindings retarget through the page's context — the takeover doctrine applied to bindings. Without an opener binding, the device-menu default stands. And a media player's takeover page now LEADS with a slim Now Playing ("when I go to Samsung TV device, shouldn't it show the same now playing element … go with the Slim version, since its now a child of the Fire TV activity") — the first concrete piece of open question 4's "richer generated device page," pulled by a real want, as prescribed.

## Sequencing — what the built slice actually is

Two engine changes, everything else inherited. (1) `detailScreen(eid)` resolves the entity back to its `CONFIG.devices` owner and, when found, supplies the device as the page: the device's `roles` (+ `dialect`, + `traits.dpad_commands`) become the page's `context`, the page is titled by the device, and the dpad role — when wired — is declared as a full `control_target` (navigation, five-key + back/home pass-through, power and volume from the roles; round 2 — a bare `dpad_passthrough` left the activity's `controls` intercepting), which swings the existing gate: physical keys to the device, the BACK/HOME strip up, tap-back/home = device / hold = Harmonium, short-press power per the v2 policy. (2) `ctxFor()` honors a new `own_context` flag: on such a page the DEVICE's context REPLACES the activity's overlay entirely (the exact inverse of the shared-controller law, and correct for the same reason — a controller is parameterized by whoever runs; a device page is about one thing). Round 3 hardened this from "the activity fills gaps" to full replacement: on Suresh's porch, the Samsung device declared no dialect, the activity's `firetv_custom` dialect leaked through the gap — and its object-form `dpad_commands` carry their own hardcoded entity, so the keys fired at the Fire TV with the page's target ignored. Under full replacement, a role the device doesn't wire is a deliberate no-op (the presence gate), and the input vocabulary can never be another device's. So on the Samsung's page, `$context.volume` is the Samsung, `cmdFor` speaks the device's own keys (or the engine defaults when the device declares none), the source chooser asks the Samsung — while one page up, the activity's wiring answers untouched. Entities no device owns keep byte-identical behavior. Corollary for configs: a pre-wired device's page is only as good as its wiring — a device with a dpad role but no dialect and no `dpad_commands` gets the generic engine verbs, which some integrations (Samsung's KEY_* vocabulary) won't understand until the device names its dialect or keys. The richer generated device controller (design-device-capabilities §8) remains later, on demand.

## Round 5 (2026-09-04, field QA): the drawer serves its opener

Three refinements from the first full hands-on pass, all pulled by reports, all fenced in `probe-takeover-chrome.mjs`:

1. **Power leads, Now Playing right below.** Suresh: "The Now Playing should be below the Power button (Porch TV)" — the round-4 NP-first order is flipped; the page opens Power, then the slim NP.

2. **The NP's trailing is the Apps drawer.** The chassis default trailing on the device page's NP navigated to `detail:` of the very entity whose page you are standing on — a button that visibly does nothing ("The Apps button on Porch TV doesn't show me the Apps"). The dnp tile now points its trail at the workspace's apps drawer when one exists (found by shape — a `drawer: true` controller generating a `type:"apps"` grid — never by name), and suppresses the trail when the workspace has none.

3. **A drawer opened from a device's page speaks the DEVICE.** His: "the menu button does show Apps — but it looks suspiciously like Fire TV Apps." The Apps drawer resolved its dialect through `ctxFor`, which overlays the ACTIVITY's context — so the Samsung's page opened the Fire TV's catalog. Opened from an ordinary page, the activity answers exactly as before.

4. **The apps drawer names its dialect.** "Menu just says Porch Apps — would be better if said FireTV Apps or Samsung Apps": `barTitle` titles a drawer that generates an apps grid by the SAME dialect resolution the tiles use (`appsDrawerDialectName`, riding `genAppTiles`' chain including the opener law, so title and tiles can never disagree) — "Fire TV-SE Apps" from the activity, "Samsung Tizen Apps" from the Samsung's page. The dialect title stands alone (no room prefix): it answers the question the room can't — whose apps these are.

Same round, Studio (fenced in `probe-variant-editor.mjs`): the browser-shaped profile (no `physical_dpad` capability, no measured viewport) previews at 480×800 instead of borrowing a handheld's 349px glass; a media tile's Variant select offers the NP styles (writing `style`, the engine's own field — blank keeps the activity's pick); only/unless gates read out loud in the tile row's subtitle ("hidden on remotes with physical_dpad"), so a working gate never reads as a dead row; and the engine keeps a Studio goto whose screen doesn't exist YET (a brand-new fork raced the debounced config push) and honors it on the next config apply — the "switch Preview as, it takes me back to the home page" report.

## Ruled and built the same day: the Media Device stock (2026-09-04)

Suresh's question, straight after the takeover shipped: the tv/music controllers serve two purposes — activity template and device control — "but we are using a made up (presumably hard-coded) entry for the Samsung TV Device Control. Would it be better to have a third stock … used in Device Mode?" And the ruling that made it immediate: "demand will be immediate because we gave the user the control over devices that he wanted but he has no way of editing it."

The answer reframed rather than added: the architecture already existed. `detailDef()` has always resolved a device page through a ladder — per-device custom copy → stock domain controller (`controllers.<domain>`, `$device`-bound) → the hard-coded `DETAIL_TILES` fallback — and climate, light, cover, fan and switch already live as domain stocks. Media player was the one domain still riding the fallback, for historical reasons only. So the build is not a third concept and needs no settings pick (the ladder IS the selection, and the existing "Custom copy for device" flow is the per-device door): `DOMAIN_STOCKS.media_player` ("Media Device", gen 1) joins the family, its tiles exactly the fallback's shape — power first, slim Now Playing with the Apps trailing (a literal `controller:apps`: the drawer is planted into every workspace, and the drawer-serves-opener law makes it the standing device's apps), transport, volume, sources, sound modes. The planter puts it in every config on Studio load, the referee heals it, Reset-to-built-in and the stock lock apply, and the takeover chrome (own_context, control_target, the strip) rides whichever rung answered — it attaches to the page, not the tile source.

Why NOT reuse controller:tv for device mode, on the record: the tv/music controllers are parameterized by an ACTIVITY (`$context` compiled from a cast of devices); a device page is parameterized by ONE DEVICE (`$device`, its own roles). One template serving both would force every tile to answer "who am I about?" two ways — the round-3 dialect leak generalized into the authoring layer. Separate parameterizations are why the leak is structurally impossible. One stock serves TVs and speakers alike (media_player entities don't declare TV-ness reliably); a device that wants a different page gets a per-device copy.

The hard-coded `DETAIL_TILES.media_player` stays as the fallback's twin (configs that predate the heal), with the twin contract fenced: `probe-media-device-stock.mjs` holds stock and fallback tile-for-tile identical, proves a user edit on the stock (hidden, restyle) reaches the generated page, proves a per-device copy wins for its entity only, and proves the takeover chrome is unmoved on the stock path. One repair rode along: the Studio's dirty-state baseline read screens only, so every controller tile wore an EDITED chip forever — invisible while controller tiles weren't editable rows, loud the day they became the point; controllers now enter the baseline.

## Round 4 of the Media Device work (2026-09-05): CH pass-through, the Lands-on select, and the wiring honored

Driven by a forum report ("was there any reason why the channel up and down would not work … we actually use those for navigating the tv") and Suresh's simpler answer to it — clone a controller, add ChUp/ChDn to KEYS PASSED TO THE DEVICE, point the activity at the clone:

1. **CH joins the pass-through for real.** The Studio's pass-through chips have offered ch_up/ch_down all along, but the engine's CH handler only ever consulted the `tuner` flag — a key the UI let you pass was silently ignored. An authored ch_up/ch_down in `control_target.pass_through` now routes CH to the device exactly like the tuner flag (the dialect's channel commands, else CHANNEL_UP/DOWN). Deliberately NOT added to the generated takeover page's list: on passthrough screens CH is the panel's only walk, so passing it stays an authored choice, never a default. `tuner: true` remains as the shorthand.

2. **The activity picks its controller** (his named bug: "No way to select a controller for the activity"). The Controller tab gained a "Lands on" select writing `a.screen` — every non-drawer controller with a resolvable shape (stocks, variants, entity-bound device copies); unbound domain stocks are excluded because their $device has nothing to bind to. And the missing engine half: an entity-bound device variant opened AS a controller surface (`controller:<id>`) now binds its $device tiles to the copy's own entity in screenOf — the minimal-TV-page-as-activity-surface flow works end to end. Context stays the activity's (no own_context): tiles pinned to the device, $context refs resolve from whoever runs.

3. **The device page volume honors the device's wiring** (his: "volume readout set to the soundbar … doesn't honor that setting"). A wired `volume_level` upgrades the generated volume row to the volume widget's ARC split — buttons → the volume role, readout → the level entity, the same spelling the activity's t_vol uses; a wired `volume` alone retargets the stepper; user edits on the stock tile ride along.

4. **Pre-wired devices in the pickers.** Every entity claimed by a device bundle wears a quiet "pre-wired" chip in every EntityPicker; a page-level device tile's Entity picker pins the pre-wired devices' page entities on top when the page has no cast of its own (the pinned group's heading is now a caller-supplied label). And label-less stock tiles read as what they are in the editor — "Source picker", "Sound modes", "Volume" — display-only friendly names (`friendlyTileName`), nothing written to the config; the chips widget joined the blue-ⓘ primer with real-world examples (tap Fire TV → the set switches to that HDMI).

Fenced: CH pass-through + the takeover page's CH-stays-the-walk negative in `probe-takeover-chrome.mjs`; the ARC split and the $device-bound controller surface in `probe-media-device-stock.mjs`.

The mechanism keeps the registry parity contract intact rather than special-casing: "none" gets a real row in the shared adapter table (both engine and studio, byte-identical) with domains:[] — so showsForDomain/showsForRoles exclude it for every domain and every role automatically (no private filter, which probe-entity-phase0 #3 forbids), and PresPanel appends the exported SHOWS_NONE constant, last. Scope, stated for the next reader: "Nothing" hides the member's own tile, not its participation in aggregate bands — a Volume-band row for a "Nothing" member still renders (that's an aggregate, governed by the Volume band switch and per-device volume:false, not by draws-as).

Fenced in probe-draw-nothing.mjs: the engine drops the device tile and the promoted control for a "none" member, keeps its volume-band row, and brings the tile back when the type is cleared; the studio cast ⚙ offers "Nothing" (last), and the registry-parity probes stay green.

### The controller mental model, agreed (2026-09-06) — and the three panel asks

Suresh stated the model and asked "does this make sense, is it true?": two kinds of controller (device pages draw one device through $device; activity surfaces draw the running activity through $context.<role>); an activity's context comes from its cast's roles; a controller shows nothing without an input; picking a cast or device to preview means "show me this surface with that as the input" — and the input decides the keys too; the preview source is freely swappable; hard-coding is possible but only for authored tiles. All true, now written up for users in docs/cookbook/how-controllers-work.md and linked from the cookbook index and the 0.87 release notes (the device-takeover-inside-an-activity upgrade: the activity's key profile used to bleed through onto a device page; now the device's own device controller is active).

What changed on the panel, all Studio-only:

1. **The control-target panel tells the truth on a device page.** It read only the AUTHORED field and said "physical keys drive the app" while the preview showed BACK | HOME — because a device page's target is derived by the engine from the device it's shown for (Navigation = its D-pad role, Power/Volume its roles; no D-pad → keys stay with the app). The panel now says exactly that, and reads the previewed device: a pre-wired D-pad device → "keys drive the device", a loose entity → "keys stay with the app". Fenced in probe-device-page-door.
2. **Device pages are flagged, both places** (his #1): the controllers tree tags them `$device`; the edit page opens with a kind pill — "$device · Device page" or "$context · Activity surface" — above the lock boundary so it never dims. Fenced both ways (probe-device-page-door, probe-lands-on).
3. **Fill in defaults** (his #2): one button in the control-target header (both editor twins) fills blank fields with the $ tokens and unions the eight standard keys into the pass-through list — never clobbering what's authored. Fenced in probe-tuner-toggle.
4. **The TV tuner switch IS the chips** (his #3 — "shouldn't we add PgUp and PgDn to the physical keys list?": exactly right). The `tuner` flag and ch_up/ch_down in the pass-through list were two spellings of one routing (input.js §5.6 honors both); two spellings is the same class of bug as two order levers. The switch now reads the chips and writes the chips; the Studio never writes the flag again; a legacy flag heals into the chips on load, reported in the status line and the audit. The engine keeps honoring the flag for hand-authored configs. Fenced in probe-tuner-toggle (ON adds, OFF removes, heal converts, flag never written).

### The final ruling on the controller preview (2026-09-07)

A day of back-and-forth ended in one sentence from Suresh: "The controller bits should stay as is... by activity overrides... but not permeate into a controller preview." That IS the "bare" design from the day before, and it stands, restored: an activity's Controller-tab overrides (Now Playing style, band switches, order, labels, volume style, speaker group/mode) stay activity overrides — they apply on the remote and in the activity card's preview, where they are edited — and they never reach the CONTROLLER editor's preview. There you are looking at the controller; the pick supplies the cast and context only, and it is bare from the moment the editor opens (no pick = the presumed owner's overrides must not leak either) until you leave it. One reader in the engine (actSurface) answers `{}` while S.pvBare is set; the Studio's controller editor sends `bare` with its impersonation for the whole visit; the activity card never does.

What this means for his screenshots, in order: the size shift between picks in the controller editor is gone (Hero for every pick, because Hero is the stock tv tile's own default); the activity card for Watch Fire TV still shows Art, because Watch Fire TV's Controller tab says Art — that is the override doing its job, and it is allowed to differ from the controller editor by design. A one-turn detour that started removing the per-activity Now Playing style was reverted before it shipped; nothing about the overrides changed. His aside stands as a separate item: the stock tv Now Playing default should probably be Art, not Hero (np_default, a gen bump) — not done, his call.

A static check finds no engine reader of an activity's surface outside actSurface. Fenced in probe-lands-on three ways (no pick bare; dressed pick bare and identical to no-pick; the activity card NOT bare with the override applied). Battery green.

### Art is the default (2026-09-07 — "I want the DEFAULT and the Auto to be ART")

The aside became a ruling: the stock TV Media Player and Music Media Player controllers now ship their Now Playing tile with `np_default: "art"` (tv gen 2, music gen 9; starter twin in step, stock history regenerated). Three things had to move together for the answer to be consistent everywhere he looked:

1. **The stock tiles** — `np_default: "art"` on `t_np` and `m_np`, so the controller editor with no dressing renders Art, and the remote renders Art wherever an activity leaves the style on Auto.
2. **Custom copies** — a copy taken while the stock said Hero carried `np_default: "hero"` of its own, so the copy's Auto stayed Hero after the stock moved. A new heal, `normalizeNpDefaults` (stocklib, after normalizeVariants), sets a copy's `np_default` to its root stock's whenever the tile has no explicit style; reported in the status line ("N custom-copy Now Playing default(s) caught up to the stock's") and in the upgrade audit. A copy with an explicit style is left alone — that is an authored choice, not a default.
3. **The Auto label** — TileRow's media variant picker said "Auto — the activity's pick", which named a mechanism, not the answer.
